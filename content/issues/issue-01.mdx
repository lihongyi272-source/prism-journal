To begin with seeing is not to begin with certainty. A film image is never only a container of story; it is a field where memory, belief, history, gesture, labor, and technology meet. PRISM's first issue asks how young writers might learn to look again, with patience and with pressure.

The essays gathered here move between close reading and cultural reflection. Some begin from a single shot. Some follow an atmosphere. Some ask what an image remembers after plot has already passed. Together, they propose criticism as an act of mediation: between films and viewers, between languages, between private sensation and public argument.

Student cinephilia is often treated as apprenticeship: a period before real criticism begins. PRISM starts from a different assumption. Young viewers are not waiting outside the conversation. They are already forming the questions through which cinema will be encountered next.

This does not mean lowering standards. It means building structures where standards can be learned, argued with, and shared. A first essay may be uneven and still contain an urgent observation. Editorial work should help that observation become clearer without flattening its original energy.

## A Journal, Not a Feed

PRISM is organized around issues, themes, and durable essays. We do not want writing to disappear into the logic of constant update. The archive matters because thinking needs a place to return to.

Our publication welcomes criticism, short essays, image notes, script fragments, translation, and statements around moving-image practice. We value poetic writing and theoretical writing, but we ask both to remain honest about what they see and what they cite.

## Translation and Mediation

Because cinema is already a cross-language art, PRISM treats translation as a central practice rather than a supporting task. Subtitles, translated essays, and bilingual discussion all belong to the same project: widening the routes by which images can be studied and shared.
